**Persona brief (from `knowledge/`, shown to the rep at the top):**
A coffee/café founder or MD is measured on net profit, growth rate, and whether they can add sites
without unit economics slipping. They know their labour number cold and know it creeps every time they
open (VOC: *"labour is without doubt our biggest challenge… tracking 39%, need 35%"*). Pain: scaling on
luck not control, a patchwork of tools that won't hold at 20 sites, thin margins under rising costs.
Nory's answer: one operating system that holds margin discipline across every new site — live P&L per
site, labour planned against forecast demand. For C-suite, lead with **peer/credibility proof** (the US
coffee cluster, scale) over a feature story. Proof resolves per send from the `coffee_cafe` set —
proof-light, never fabricated.

## Discovery goals (probe on the first live conversation — not in the cold email)
- **Incumbent + contract end date** — contract expiry is the #1 compelling event (`_signal_correlation.md`);
  ask "when does that run to?" on call 1. Not a first-touch hook (we don't know it yet).
- **POS fit** — confirm early; a POS mismatch is a common DQ.
- **Owner = decision-maker?** — for SMB coffee the owner *is* the C-suite; for a group, ask "does this
  sit with you or at board level?" (`_objections.md` #4).
- If on **XtraChef / an inventory-first tool** → lead the US displacement story (`proof_library.md`).

## Copy

### Email 1 — Day 1 — `CUSTOM` (agent generates per account)
Example for an account with signals *Series A + 4 new cafés this year*:
> **Subject:** 4 new cafés + holding the margin
>
> Hi {{first}} — congrats on the raise. 4 new cafés in a year is real momentum, and it's usually the
> point where margin gets harder to hold: labour % and COGS drift site by site, and by the time it
> shows in the P&L it's already banked. Growth on luck, not control.
>
> Nory is the system a few US coffee groups run to protect net profit while they scale — one live P&L
> per site, labour planned to forecast demand.
>
> Worth 20 minutes on how they keep the margin as the estate grows? {{rep_first}}

### Email 2 — Day 4 — templated (labour % as you scale)
> **Subject:** labour % as you add sites
> Hi {{first}} — most coffee founders I speak to know their labour number cold, and know it creeps
> every time they open. The real question is whether you can add the next handful of sites without it
> drifting past target. Nory plans labour to forecast demand per site, so the discipline scales with
> you. Worth 20 minutes? {{rep_first}}

### Email 3 — Day 9 — templated (peer credibility)
> ⚠️ **Coffee proof gap:** `proof_library.md` has **no hard coffee labour number** (Black Sheep figures
> unpublished). For C-suite, resolve proof to the **US coffee logo cluster** (peer credibility) plus a
> **forecasting-accuracy** line, or a general Nory outcome — never a fabricated coffee %.
>
> **Subject:** how peer coffee groups hold margin while scaling
> Hi {{first}} — we're already the system behind several US coffee groups ({{coffee_cafe_proof.cluster}}),
> scaling on the same two cost lines you're watching. Forecasting lands within ~1–2% of actuals, so
> labour and ordering plan to real demand rather than gut. Happy to show how they hold margin as they
> grow. Worth 20 min? {{rep_first}}

### Email 4 — Day 14 — templated (breakup)

## The flow — 12 days · 3 calls · 5 emails · 2 LinkedIn

> Day spacing is still v0's guess — he hasn't given days yet. Flagged again below.

**Day 1 · Email 1 — BESPOKE SLOT (agent).** Signal + social proof, per account: his Custom 1,
automated. Hook priority is his own rule (rep's notes → Propel → web → role+scale, never fabricated).

**Day 1 · Call 1 — REWRITTEN.** The opening line is account-specific and comes from the brief's
`why_now`, the same fact Email 1 opens on:

> "Hi {first}, Will from Nory. You'll not know me, and this is a cold call, but not a list one.
> {the agent's why-now, in your words: 'I saw the Carlisle site opened in February', 'saw you're
> five pubs across South East London now'}. How's that going?"

**Then stop talking.** The call is their answer. Nothing about Nory until they ask.

Only when they ask what you do, one sentence, no product names:

> "We give multi-site operators one live view of labour and stock per site, so a bad week is
> visible while you can still do something about it."

Close: *"Worth twenty minutes with someone who'll show you it on your own numbers?"*

**Day 2 · LinkedIn connect** — no message.

**Day 3 · Email 2 — BESPOKE SLOT (agent): the ROI email.** His labour-exposure calculation
(sites × hours × £13 × 1.25 × 360, 5% recoverable), computed per account and framed as he frames it:
estimates, not actuals, "the shape tends to hold at your scale". One question close.

**Day 5 · Call 2 + voicemail** (under 20 seconds):
> "Hi {first}, Will at Nory. I sent you the labour numbers for {company}: the rough shape of what
> inefficient deployment costs at your scale. Worth a quick look before my next try, Thursday."

**Day 6 · Email 3 — Location performance** (his, unchanged — he marked this one good):
> **Subject:** your best site, everywhere
>
> {first}, what if your worst site ran like your best? Usually the gap comes down to systems, not
> people. Nory takes what works at your best site and runs it everywhere, automatically. Operators
> see 5 to 11% reductions in labour, and the gap between best and worst site closes. Anything in
> that for {company}?

**Day 8 · Call 3.**

**Day 9 · Email 4 — 2027 labour exposure** (REBUILT around the law change):
> **Subject:** cutting shifts in 2027
>
> {first}, most operators absorbed April's rise and are walking into 2027 carrying the same labour
> exposure.
>
> Two things land on top of it. The Low Pay Commission is projecting £13.02 to £13.34 for next
> April, not confirmed until the Budget, but that's the planning range. And the Employment Rights
> Act's guaranteed-hours and shift-notice rules are on the government's 2027 timeline, timings
> still to be confirmed.
>
> Both make cutting shifts a harder lever than it was this year.
>
> Do you know which of your sites carries that exposure?

> ⚠️ **Do not send this email to Northern Ireland accounts.** The Employment Rights Act is Great
> Britain scope; NI employment law is devolved and this has not been verified for NI. One account in
> his current batch is affected (Deanes, Belfast). For NI, drop the second paragraph's ERA sentence
> and run the email on the projection alone.

**Day 11 · LinkedIn DM** (his favourite step, kept verbatim):
> "Sent you a couple of notes on labour exposure ahead of April. One number in there worth 2
> minutes. Happy to share how {similar operator} handled it."

`{similar operator}` is **supplied per account in the brief** — matched on vertical and scale from
the 12 verified brands, never picked at random, always quoted with its published qualifier.

**Day 12 · Email 5 — breakup** (his, unchanged):
> **Subject:** timing
>
> {first}, looks like the timing's off and now's not the time to speak. Will this be among your
> priorities in 6 to 9 months?

## The 5 objections we actually hear

### 1. "It's not the right time" — #1 objection (50+ calls, all segments/roles)
Means one of: (a) a genuine competing priority, (b) no internal sponsor, or (c) the pain isn't sharp
enough yet.
**What moves it:**
- Don't push for the meeting — push for the *reason*: **"What would have to be true for it to be the
  right time?"** surfaces the real blocker.
- If they name a project ("mid-rollout"), ask when it ends and **book a dated callback** — beats a
  vague "call me in a few months."
- If they can't name a reason, the real objection is "you haven't given me a reason to care yet" — go
  back to the pain.
- **Verbatim:** *"We've changed the brand. We're going in the right direction. Yeah, it's just not the
  right time for me to talk now."* — MD, casual dining · *"There are many projects going on. So this is
  not the right time to do it yet."* — MD

### 2. "We're happy with what we have" / locked into an incumbent
Clusters on **Ops Managers + MDs** (SMB & mid-market) who've bought an incumbent (UKI: Fourth, Harri,
Deputy, Kobas).
**What moves it:**
- Name the incumbent, ask **"What's working well about [X]?"** — surfaces the gaps better than pitching.
- **Get the contract end date on the first call:** "When does that contract run to?" A prospect who
  tells you is already shopping.
- **Kobas** specifically came up (MM) as a "we think it already covers food cost" blocker — counter by
  showing what Nory does that it doesn't: **real-time labour scheduling against forecast, not static rotas.**

### 3. "Unclear ROI / not sure it pays for itself" — **finance / C-suite**
CFO/FD objections cluster on ROI *tangibility* — they want a number, not a story. **"It'll save you
time" does not land with finance.**
**What moves it — the Labour Assessment (primary tool):**
- Used on MM/ENT calls (Fuller's, Cornerstone) to produce a **defensible £ ROI before commercials**.
  Turns "we think we can save you money" into "based on your current 34% labour cost and X sites,
  here's the modelled saving."
- Format finance responds to = **actual £ figures tied to their P&L, not percentages.** Real output
  (Cornerstone call): *"labour savings of £40k, GP variance saving of £11k."*

### 4. "We need exec / operator buy-in first" — ENT / franchise groups
Not about Nory — internal politics: approval forums, franchise agreements dictating systems, business
partners operating independently.
**What moves it:**
- **Map the decision process on call 1:** "Who else needs to be involved before a decision like this?"
  — don't wait to be told there's a committee.
- Get the champion to name the internal **forum + next meeting date** → that's your compelling event.
- **Franchise trap:** the national decision-maker is rarely on the first call. Ask directly: **"Are you
  the right person, or does this sit at group/brand level?"**

### 5. "GM rotas need human judgment — this won't work for us" — Ops, scheduling automation (MM)
Ops Managers resist anything that feels like it replaces manager discretion.
**The counter:** Nory doesn't replace judgment, it gives better inputs. **"Your GM still makes the
call — they just make it with actual sales-forecast data instead of gut feel."**

## US adaptation (do not skip)
The corpus is UKI. For US outbound:
- **Swap the incumbent names:** UKI Fourth / Harri / Deputy / Kobas → US **Toast, HotSchedules (Fourth
  US), Crunchtime, XtraChef**, and similar. Confirm the US stack per account before naming a competitor.
- **Proven US displacement: XtraChef → Nory** (Grand Traverse Pie Co., Backal Hospitality both switched
  after XtraChef under-delivered). When a US prospect is on **XtraChef or an inventory-first tool**, lead
  with the displacement story (`proof_library.md` US section) — it's a real, repeatable win.
- **Currency:** the ROI figures above (e.g. the Cornerstone £40k/£11k) are real **UK £** call outputs — for
  US, **model the $ on the prospect's own cost base**; never relabel a UK £ figure as a US $.
- Flag on any US deliverable that objection handling is UKI-derived until we have US call volume.

## How cadence agents use this
- **First touch / emails:** pre-empt objection #1 by making the pain sharp and time-bound (a signal =
  the reason it's the right time *now*); pre-empt #3 (finance) by leading with a **$** outcome (US: modelled on their costs), not "save
  time."
- **Call scripts:** carry the "what moves it" lines verbatim as the objection-handling track.
- **Finance cadences specifically:** offer the **Labour Assessment** as the low-friction ask — it's the
  proven ROI-tangibility tool.

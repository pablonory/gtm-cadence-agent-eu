## C-suite (MDs / Owners / CEOs / COOs)
Talk in **percentages and £ impact**; often already resigned to the problem. COO pain is subtler — the
data doesn't tell the truth, or wins don't feel like wins.

- *"Our labour is without doubt still the biggest challenge. We got 33 percent at the plow last week but
  we're tracking about 39 percent. If we can get that down to 35 percent that would be a huge benefit."*
  — Adam, MD (pub)
- *"My labour costs have all gone up. My energy bills have all gone up. My cost of the stock has all gone
  up."* — Richard, MD (casual dining)
- *"The one that screams out to me is our operating costs. We've almost given up trying to control labor.
  It's so high."* — Lewis, MD (QSR)
- *"Food mainly and labour — cost of goods and cost of labour improvements using forecast capability,
  building schedules."* — MD (coffee shop)
- *"Our labor costs were way below our target. But it doesn't really feel like a big victory because our
  revenue was also lower as well."* — Jeremy Cohen, COO
- *"The food cost is actually very good — about 28 percent — and labor is about 34 percent. The rent is
  the biggest issue, about 15 percent, which is a lot."* — Eduardo, CEO (casual dining) *(knows his
  numbers cold — high-quality prospect signal)*

## Finance (data you can't trust — the accuracy pain)
Lands hardest in finance conversations: figures that don't match, P&L inaccuracies affecting quarter
reviews, "which number is right?".

- *"The inaccuracies were obviously affecting the P&L and affecting our overview for Q1 and Q2. We just
  want a system that gives us accurate GP, accurate stocks, accurate labour."* — COO-level (casual dining)
- *"It could go either way because if the finance team tells me this food cost is the right one I'm happy
  to go with that. But now there's a difference of one or two percent so I always get questions like
  which one is right?"* — Akshay, Ops Manager (casual dining) *(the "which number is right?" pain — a
  strong demo hook for finance)*

## Ops (Directors / Managers — the champions who live it daily)
Feel the pain in **hours lost, manual workarounds, broken integrations**.

- *"Till's talking to the labour system — that would be honestly game changing for us. It'd help all the
  figures and give the operators more time to do what they're meant to be doing."* — James, Operations
  Director (pub group)
- *"We need to better understand our reporting. We need to start being able to predict and forecast
  labour costs… and we've tried to do that using GPT Excel sheets and it's just not…"* — James,
  Operations Director (hotel F&B) *(cuts off mid-sentence — the pain is real)*
- *"We want stock depletion, staff costs — I shouldn't be spending hours manually figuring all this out.
  It's a waste of my time. It's not profit leading."* — Chris Edwards, Ops Manager
- *"One of my jobs this week is to see if I can make excel work to the minute rather than every 15
  minutes to try and bring down the costs."* — Christopher Alexander, Ops Manager (pub)

## The three pain patterns to anchor on
1. **Labour % creeping beyond target** — most universal + acute. Nearly every MD/owner has a number in
   their head (33/35/39%) and knows they're over it. → lead here for C-suite/Ops.
2. **Manual workarounds eating Ops time** — Excel, GPT sheets, 15-minute intervals, hours of
   reconciliation. Champions feel it personally. → the Ops hook.
3. **Data you can't trust** — food-cost figures that don't match, P&L inaccuracies hitting quarter
   reviews, "which number is right?". → the Finance hook.

## How cadence agents use this
- **First touch:** open on the pattern that matches the persona (labour % → C-suite; manual time →
  Ops; "which number is right?" → Finance), echoing the buyer's own phrasing — not marketing language.
- **Call opener:** frame the pain as their words ("most ops leaders I speak to are still doing this in
  Excel to the minute — is that you, or have you solved it?").
- Prefer the buyer's plain words ("labour is our biggest challenge", "waste of my time") over Nory's
  polished terms.

## The golden rule (what actually moves deals — all verticals)
Deal-moving proof is always one of these three shapes:
1. **Dated COL% reduction tied to a named / "similar" brand** — not "up to X%", but *"in Q3 alone, COL
   dropped 13.5%."*
2. **Forecasting accuracy within 1–2% of actuals** — kills the "your AI is a black box" objection before
   it lands.
3. **Saving translated to their site count** — the UK benchmark (a 10-location group ~£50k/year) is
   native here: quote it directly, or model the £/€ figure on the prospect's own cost base via the
   Labour Assessment for precision.
> When a **CFO / Finance lead is in the room, lead with the P&L translation** — the **% saved** plus a $
> figure **modelled on their own cost base** (US), not the feature story. (Rocket Restaurants call: Magnus led labour + COGS framing for a CFO →
> Labour Assessment follow-up booked — ties to the finance ROI-tangibility pattern in `_objections.md`.)

## Merge-field resolution (how cadences use this)
- `{{<vertical>_proof.brand}}` / `.metric` / `.result` / `.timeframe` resolve to a row above **matched to
  the account's vertical**. Never quote a QSR/FSR win to a coffee prospect or vice-versa.
- `.secondary` — the second metric of a "double" (e.g. the **GP-added** half of the QSR COL%+GP% story).
  Resolve only when the chosen proof row has one; otherwise the copy collapses to the single result.
- `.forecast_accuracy` — the ~97%/within-1–2% forecast-accuracy proof (the main *quantified* proof for
  proof-light Coffee/Café; also usable elsewhere).
- `.cluster` — a **logo cluster** line (not a number), for proof-light or logo-led sends (e.g. Coffee/Café
  "already in four US coffee groups"). Used where no quantified proof exists.
- One proof per message. Prefer a **named or "similar" brand + dated % + £/€**; if the exact brand isn't
  quotable (anonymised), use "a similar [vertical] brand".
- If the vertical has no hard number (**Coffee**), fall back to a general Nory outcome (`knowledge/product.md`)
  and mark the copy "proof pending", rather than fabricating.
